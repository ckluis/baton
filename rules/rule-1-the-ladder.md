## 1. The Tiers

Work is routed on one ordered list of three **tiers**. A tier is the unit of
escalation. There is no other.

| # | tier | for |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `cheap` | Mechanical and verifiable by command: run the suite, collect coverage numbers, apply a named rename, check a link list, resolve a roster. The fastest model the harness offers. **Assignment only — escalation never lands here.** |
| 1 | `frontier` | **The default.** Everything with judgment in it: implementation against a spec, diagnosis, design, verification, planning, adjudication, synthesis. The most capable model the harness offers, at its highest effort. Nothing above it runs unattended. |
| 2 | `human` | Not a spawn: a question in the inbox (§10) with the evidence beside it, batched at the gate (§8). Where frontier stops — a second failure, a contradiction no adjudicator can settle on the evidence, a criterion no execution can meet (§9.2) — and where authority was always going to be needed. |

`rung` is the field's name in graphs, envelopes, persona cards and the ledger,
kept so that nothing which reads them changes; its values are `0`, `1`, or
`n/a` for an event row (§7.2). Six rungs collapsed to these three when the
premise they priced changed. Escalating from `sonnet/medium` to `sonnet/high`
bought more thinking before a bigger model because the bigger model was the
expensive part of a run. When the most capable model is the sensible default
for anything with judgment in it, the ladder has two agent tiers and one
human, and a run's cost discipline lives in *what* is done at the cheap tier
and *how much* is asked of a person — both of which the ledger still measures.

The binding of tiers to models belongs to the harness and the operator: router
§1's `CHEAP` and `FRONTIER` name them, and the ledger's `model` and `effort`
columns record what actually ran (§7). A rule that named a model would be
wrong within a year; a rule that names a property of the work is not.
