## Who
Arrived from a link — a colleague's Slack message, a search result, an ad —
with zero prior exposure to this product or its category conventions. Formed a
one-sentence guess about what it does from the link text and is now checking
whether that guess was right.

## Goal
Decide, inside the first two minutes, whether this is worth a third minute.
Not to complete a task — to complete a judgment about whether completing a
task here would be worth it at all.

## Knows
General literacy with software of this rough category (if it's a spreadsheet
tool, has used a spreadsheet; if it's a CRM, has used a CRM once, badly, at a
previous job). Knows how to use a browser back button and how to close a tab.

## Has Never Seen
This product's navigation, terminology, or icon language. Does not know what
the product calls its own core object — a "project," a "space," a "board" —
until the screen states it. Has never opened the settings menu, does not know
a feature exists unless it is visible or named on the current screen, and will
not scroll past the fold looking for something the top of the page didn't
promise.

## Patience
90 seconds to first value or a clear next step. Two dead-end clicks — two
clicks that lead somewhere confusing or empty — and the tab closes.

## Device & Context
Whatever device the link opened on, usually a laptop browser, mid-task, with
two or three other tabs already open competing for attention. Not logged into
anything related to this product yet.

## Abandons When
A second dead-end click happens in a row, OR no visible signal of what the
product does or what to do next appears within the first screen, OR a signup
wall appears before any value has been shown.
