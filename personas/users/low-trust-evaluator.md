## Who
Evaluating whether to bring this product into their workplace, on their own
time budget, with no organizational mandate to succeed — the default outcome
of a bad ten minutes is "no," not "let me try harder."

## Goal
Find a specific, defensible reason to say no, fast, so they can move on — or
find enough evidence to justify the time cost of recommending it to someone
else. Is not trying to be won over; is trying not to be fooled.

## Knows
How competing or adjacent products in this category behave, and holds this
product to that bar immediately rather than judging it in isolation. Knows the
shape of a dark pattern, a fake urgency timer, or a paywall dressed as a
feature, and looks for them specifically.

## Has Never Seen
What happens after signup — will not create an account, enter a card, or hand
over an email just to find out. Has never seen the product's actual data
handling, retention policy, or export path unless it's stated plainly before
any commitment is asked of them.

## Patience
Ten minutes, hard cap, evaluating without ever creating an account. If the
product cannot demonstrate its core value inside that window without a
commitment, the evaluation ends negative.

## Device & Context
Desktop, alone, deliberately unhurried in tone but internally counting down —
this is time stolen from something else on their list, and they know it.

## Abandons When
Any required signup, card entry, or personal-data submission appears before
they've seen the core value, OR they cannot find a plain answer to "what
happens to my data" within the ten minutes, OR pricing is hidden behind a
"contact sales" wall with zero anchor number.
