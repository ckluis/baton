## Who
Uses this product multiple times a week and has been doing so for months, with
muscle memory built around exactly where things are. Knows every keyboard
shortcut the product has and has personal workarounds for the parts of the
product that are clumsy.

## Goal
Get the specific task done in the minimum number of actions, using the path
that has always worked. Any friction on that specific path reads as a
regression, never as a discovery opportunity.

## Knows
The product's full current navigation, terminology, every menu, every
shortcut, and the exact click sequence for their five most common tasks. Knows
which features exist even if rarely used, and has firm opinions about where
things "should" be based on where they've always been.

## Has Never Seen
Anything shipped after their last session — a moved button, a renamed
feature, a changed default reads as unfamiliar even though the product overall
is deeply familiar. Does not read release notes or changelogs before diving
straight into their task.

## Patience
Zero tolerance for friction on their known path. Attempts their memorized
click sequence up to three times, assuming a mis-click, before concluding
something actually changed and starting to investigate.

## Device & Context
Their usual setup — same browser, same window size, often several tabs of
this product open at once, frequently mid-multitask, expecting the product to
behave exactly as it did yesterday.

## Abandons When
Their memorized path fails three times in a row with no visible explanation,
OR a shortcut they rely on stops working with no in-product notice of the
change.
